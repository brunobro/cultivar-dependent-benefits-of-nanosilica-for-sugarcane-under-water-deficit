# Cultivar-dependent benefits of nanosilica for sugarcane under water deficit: a PCA–fuzzy multivariate performance index

Water deficit limits sugarcane productivity, and nanosilica (nSi) has been proposed to mitigate it, but its effects are usually assessed variable by variable. We evaluated nSi under water deficit in two cultivars (RB027040 and RB867515) of plant cane (2025) and first ratoon (2026) in a factorial arrangement with four blocks, and summarized 21 morphophysiological, biochemical and technological variables in a multivariate performance score. The score was obtained by a zero-order Takagi–Sugeno fuzzy inference system fed by three principal components and tuned by particle swarm optimization, and was validated by leave-one-block-out cross-validation. Out-of-fold scores were analyzed by factorial permutation tests, and the net gain from nSi by a stress efficiency index and a difference-in-differences. In the multivariate analysis, water deficit and nSi were the largest sources of variation in both seasons. Stress without nSi gave the lowest scores (31.5 to 36.7), while nSi raised the score under stress by 12.3 to 45.1 points, and the net gain was positive in all cultivar × season combinations (+8.9 to +25.7 points). In RB027040, stressed plants with nSi matched well-watered plants with nSi. The water × nSi interaction was significant only in 2026, and effects on cane yield were heterogeneous and imprecise. The evidence for drought mitigation by nSi is therefore consistent but preliminary, and fuzzy composite indices should be validated against simpler alternatives.
